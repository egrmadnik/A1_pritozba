## 📡 Situation as of September 9, 2026

On Wednesday, September 9, 2026, after several days of complete unavailability, the internet connection **suddenly resumed normal operation**.

The existing **ZTE888B** modem re-established full **4G/LTE and 5G connectivity** without any local modifications or configuration changes. Data transfer is back at normal broadband speeds, the signal is stable, and the modem's `Network` LED is no longer stuck blinking blue in 3G mode, but indicates active LTE/5G operation.

## 📦 A1 Response: Silent Resolution and Bureaucratic Irony

Even though the issue was unmistakably rectified on the carrier's network side, A1's handling of the case provides a textbook example of disconnect between technical reality and customer support workflows:

### 1. The Promised Call from Technical Support Never Happened
Despite customer support explicitly promising a **call-back from technical/engineering personnel**, **no technician has called** to date, nor has any explanation regarding the outage been provided.

### 2. Dispatching a Replacement Modem Without Explanation
Instead of technical troubleshooting or communication, A1 dispatched a **new replacement ZTE888B modem via postal mail** without prior notice. This occurred despite my detailed technical ticket explicitly stating and proving that local hardware was functional (active 3G connection, SIM card detected, local resets attempted multiple times, ~200 m distance to the base station).

### 3. Official Notice Demanding Return of the Working Modem
I received an official email notification requesting that I:
* **Return the existing modem**—which has worked properly all along and is currently delivering full 4G/5G service,
* And **install the newly dispatched modem** of the exact same model?!

## 🔍 Technical Analysis: Conclusive Proof of a Network-Side Fault

This turn of events completely vindicates the initial complaint:

1. **Undeniable Proof of Functional Hardware:** The fact that the existing modem restored full 4G/5G connectivity on its own while the replacement unit was still in transit at the post office conclusively proves that **there was never anything wrong with the local customer-premises hardware**.
2. **Silent Network-Side Fix:** The problem was located entirely on A1's cellular network infrastructure (base station sector outage, radio access parameter fault, or core network routing/reselection issue restricting the device to 3G). The network team evidently resolved the fault silently.
3. **Flawed Support Procedures:** Instead of escalating the issue to the Network Operations Center (NOC / RAN team), frontline support defaulted to blindly shipping replacement hardware. This generated unnecessary shipping costs for the operator and unwarranted administrative friction for the subscriber.

## ❓ Open Questions for A1

1. **Why was the promised engineering call-back never fulfilled?** Subscribers paying full price are entitled to know the root cause of a multi-day total service disruption.
2. **Why does customer support disregard structured technical diagnostics?** Had the support center acted on the provided evidence (functional 3G, valid SIM, 200 m proximity), the case would have been routed immediately to network engineering rather than warehouse logistics.
3. **What is the rationale for swapping identical, functional hardware?** Disconnecting a verified, stable modem and deploying an identical unit introduces pointless risks (new IMEI authorization delays, potential provisioning hiccups) and forces an unnecessary trip to the post office.
4. **Who is accountable for the domestic disruption caused?** Subscriptions are billed at 100%, yet a multi-day outage produces needless stress and friction at home, on top of lost time.
